# Pedagogical AI Guidelines

Version: 1.0

## Purpose and scope

This document defines the pedagogical principles that govern every AI-assisted training feature in AI Training Studio Pro. It establishes what useful, responsible, and professionally credible AI-supported learning content means for trainers and learners.

The AI is a pedagogical assistant. It helps trainers design, structure, improve, and evaluate learning experiences. It does not replace professional judgment, subject-matter expertise, regulatory verification, or trainer accountability.

These guidelines apply to all future AI-assisted lessons, courses, assessments, scenarios, visuals, presentations, learning packages, and evaluations.

## 1. Human control principle

AI may:

- propose content and alternatives;
- assist analysis and drafting;
- structure learning material;
- improve clarity, consistency, and accessibility;
- identify questions or assumptions for trainer review.

AI must never:

- decide final pedagogical choices;
- publish, distribute, or certify content automatically;
- modify learning material without explicit approval;
- present a suggestion as an authoritative trainer decision;
- remove the trainer's responsibility for accuracy and suitability.

The trainer remains responsible for learning objectives, content selection, instructional strategy, assessment validity, regulatory compliance, and final approval. AI-generated material is always a proposal until the trainer accepts it.

## 2. Training-first approach

AI output must be designed as training, not merely as generated text. Every substantial proposal should contribute to one or more defined learning outcomes.

Priority must be given to:

- clear and observable learning objectives;
- the needs and professional context of the target audience;
- acquisition and demonstration of competence;
- practical transfer to real situations;
- appropriate practice and feedback;
- assessment aligned with the objectives.

Fluency, length, and stylistic polish do not compensate for weak instructional value. Content that reads well but does not help learners understand, practise, or demonstrate competence is pedagogically insufficient.

## 3. Pedagogical structure

AI-assisted content should consider the following before proposing a learning structure:

- target audience and professional role;
- prerequisite knowledge and experience;
- intended learning objectives;
- difficulty and autonomy level;
- available learning duration;
- delivery setting and teaching method;
- practical constraints and required resources;
- evaluation method and success criteria.

When information is missing, the AI should identify the gap or state a reviewable assumption. It must not silently invent learner characteristics, prerequisites, duration, or assessment requirements.

The structure should progress logically from prior knowledge to new understanding, guided practice, independent application, and assessment where appropriate.

## 4. Course generation principles

Future lesson and course generation should be capable of supporting this reference structure:

- title;
- learning objectives;
- introduction and relevance to the learner;
- theoretical or conceptual content;
- practical examples;
- guided and independent exercises;
- evaluation;
- summary and key takeaways.

This is a reference, not a mandatory template for every course. The sequence, depth, and components should adapt to the training type, audience, duration, delivery method, and risk level.

Generated courses should:

- maintain alignment between objectives, activities, and evaluation;
- distinguish essential knowledge from enrichment;
- use examples that reflect the learner's context;
- provide opportunities for active participation;
- avoid unnecessary repetition and content inflation;
- make prerequisites and expected outcomes explicit.

## 5. Assessment principles

AI-generated assessments must measure the stated learning objectives rather than superficial recall alone. Each assessment proposal should consider:

- alignment with one or more objectives;
- appropriate difficulty and cognitive demand;
- coverage of essential knowledge or competence;
- variety suited to the training context;
- clear wording and a defensible expected answer;
- explanations or feedback for answers;
- avoidance of ambiguity, trick questions, and unintended clues.

Supported assessment forms may include:

- multiple-choice questions;
- true-or-false questions;
- practical scenarios;
- open questions.

Distractors in multiple-choice questions should be plausible without being deceptive. True-or-false statements should test meaningful understanding. Scenarios should include enough context for a justified decision. Open questions should include expected criteria or guidance for evaluation.

AI-generated scores, pass thresholds, or claims of competence require trainer validation and must not be treated as certification decisions automatically.

## 6. Professional training context

AI-assisted content must adapt to the realities of professional learning, including:

- corporate training;
- security training;
- emergency response training;
- compliance training;
- technical training.

Professional accuracy has priority over creativity. Examples, terminology, equipment, roles, procedures, and constraints should reflect the relevant workplace. Content should support practical decision-making and transfer without oversimplifying high-risk responsibilities.

The AI should distinguish between general educational examples and instructions intended for operational use. It must not imply that generic generated material replaces organizational procedures, qualified instruction, supervised practice, or required certification.

## 7. Source reliability

AI-assisted content must clearly distinguish among:

- user-provided sources;
- validated references;
- generated suggestions;
- stated assumptions.

Claims should remain traceable to their source category. A generated suggestion must not be presented as if it came from an official or validated reference.

The AI must not invent:

- laws or regulations;
- standards or official guidance;
- certification requirements;
- accreditation claims;
- statistics, references, or quotations.

When reliable support is unavailable, the AI should express uncertainty and request verification. Trainers should be able to identify which claims require subject-matter, legal, regulatory, or organizational review.

## 8. Adaptation to learners

AI-assisted content should support at least these broad learner levels:

- beginner;
- intermediate;
- expert.

Adaptation may affect:

- vocabulary and terminology;
- conceptual complexity;
- amount of explanation and scaffolding;
- examples and professional situations;
- exercise difficulty;
- degree of learner autonomy;
- expected analysis and decision-making.

Adaptation must preserve the underlying learning objective unless the trainer explicitly changes it. Simplification should reduce unnecessary complexity, not remove essential safety, technical, or compliance information.

The AI should avoid unsupported assumptions about ability based on age, role, language, culture, disability, or educational background. Inclusive alternatives should be proposed when learner needs are known.

## 9. Multimedia generation principles

Future AI-generated images, diagrams, scenarios, and presentations must serve an identifiable learning objective. Visual or multimedia material should help learners:

- understand a concept or relationship;
- recognize equipment, states, or hazards;
- follow a process or sequence;
- analyse a situation;
- remember essential information;
- practise a professional decision.

Multimedia must not be included solely for decoration. It should be accurate, relevant, legible, accessible, and appropriate to the audience.

Generated visuals should be reviewed for misleading details, impossible configurations, incorrect labels, unsafe demonstrations, stereotypes, and cultural bias. Important information should not depend exclusively on visual perception when an accessible alternative is needed.

## 10. AI explanations

AI-assisted proposals should be explainable. When useful to the trainer, the AI should identify:

- why it proposes a structure, activity, example, or assessment;
- which learning objective the proposal serves;
- which learner or context information influenced it;
- what assumptions were used;
- what uncertainties or verification needs remain.

Explanations should be concise, relevant, and understandable. They must not create false confidence or imply access to evidence that was not supplied or validated.

Transparency enables the trainer to accept, revise, partially accept, regenerate, or reject a proposal with informed judgment.

## 11. Security and compliance training

Training involving legal requirements, safety procedures, emergency actions, security operations, or other sensitive instructions requires a higher standard of care.

AI-assisted content in these domains must prioritize:

- factual and procedural accuracy;
- current and applicable requirements;
- official and authoritative sources;
- clear jurisdiction and organizational scope;
- explicit human and subject-matter validation;
- unambiguous safety-critical wording.

The AI must identify uncertainty, conflicting sources, missing jurisdiction, or potentially outdated information. It must not invent operational steps or convert general knowledge into authoritative procedure without validation.

AI output must not replace approved procedures, qualified instruction, practical supervision, risk assessment, or legally required certification. High-risk content should be reviewed before learners can rely on it.

## 12. Localization

AI-assisted content should support:

- language adaptation;
- cultural adaptation;
- regional terminology;
- regional legal or professional requirements.

Localization must preserve the underlying learning objective and assessment intent. Translation should not silently change technical meaning, risk level, units, roles, or performance criteria.

Regional requirements must be verified for the relevant jurisdiction. A translated regulation or procedure must not be assumed to apply in another region. Examples and scenarios should avoid cultural stereotypes and should remain understandable and professionally appropriate for the intended learners.

## 13. Pedagogical quality criteria

AI-assisted content should be evaluated against these criteria:

- **Accuracy:** Claims, procedures, terminology, and examples are correct and appropriately qualified.
- **Clarity:** Learners can understand what they are expected to know or do.
- **Relevance:** Content contributes to the objectives and fits the learner's professional context.
- **Learner engagement:** Activities encourage attention, reflection, participation, or meaningful practice.
- **Practical applicability:** Learners can connect knowledge to realistic tasks, decisions, or situations.
- **Assessment quality:** Evaluation fairly measures the stated objectives with clear criteria and useful feedback.
- **Consistency:** Objectives, explanations, activities, terminology, difficulty, and assessment remain aligned throughout the training.

Quality should be judged for the complete learning experience, not only for individual passages. A polished output that fails an essential criterion must be revised before approval.

## 14. Relationship with AI Architecture

`PEDAGOGICAL_AI_GUIDELINES.md` defines **what good training content means** for AI Training Studio Pro. It governs pedagogical intent, professional quality, learner adaptation, assessment, transparency, and trainer responsibility.

`AI_ARCHITECTURE.md` defines **how AI capabilities are technically implemented**. It governs application boundaries, providers, tasks, requests, context, results, streaming, security, and human approval mechanisms.

The documents are complementary:

- technical compliance does not guarantee pedagogical quality;
- pedagogical quality requirements do not authorize bypassing technical architecture;
- every AI-assisted training feature must satisfy both documents.

If a proposed feature creates tension between pedagogical needs and technical safeguards, the conflict must be reviewed rather than resolved by weakening either contract silently.

## 15. Future AI modules

### Lesson Generator

The Lesson Generator should produce coherent learning sequences aligned with the audience, prerequisites, objectives, duration, teaching method, practice needs, and evaluation strategy. It should distinguish essential content from optional enrichment and explain important structural choices.

### Quiz Generator

The Quiz Generator should create varied, objective-aligned questions with defensible answers and useful explanations. It should support appropriate difficulty, avoid ambiguity, and provide enough information for trainer review before use.

### Scenario Generator

The Scenario Generator should create realistic professional situations that require learners to interpret information, make decisions, or apply procedures. Scenarios should state assumptions, avoid unsafe or impossible situations, and include debriefing points or evaluation criteria.

### SCORM Assistant

The SCORM Assistant should help organize approved learning content into a coherent learner journey. It should preserve objectives, sequencing, completion expectations, assessment intent, accessibility, and trainer-approved material. Packaging must not be treated as evidence of pedagogical quality or compliance by itself.

### Presentation Generator

The Presentation Generator should turn approved content into a clear instructional narrative. Slides should support facilitation and learner understanding rather than reproduce dense documents. Visuals, examples, pacing, and speaker guidance should serve explicit objectives.

### Training Evaluation Assistant

The Training Evaluation Assistant should help trainers examine learner achievement, assessment quality, practical transfer, and opportunities for improvement. It should distinguish observed evidence from interpretation, avoid unsupported conclusions, and leave final evaluation decisions to the trainer.

## Governance

Future AI-powered training features must reference these guidelines during design and review. New training domains or pedagogical capabilities should extend this document when they introduce materially different quality, safety, localization, or assessment requirements.

Changes to these guidelines require pedagogical review. They must remain consistent with the product vision and the normative AI architecture.
